# BenchSpec Catalogue Guide

## Purpose

BenchSpec publishes selected specialist technical equipment that benefits from precise identification and searchable technical detail.

The catalogue exists to:

- help obscure equipment be discovered through search;
- give technical buyers confidence in exactly what is being offered;
- present actual condition and verification clearly;
- route purchase traffic to the current commerce channel, initially eBay.

BenchSpec is not positioned as a generic surplus dealer or mass-market ecommerce store.

## Brand position

BenchSpec is a specialist source of technical equipment.

Stock may include:

- new surplus;
- unused / NOS equipment;
- decommissioned equipment;
- pre-owned equipment;
- reclaimed components;
- selectively inspected or tested equipment.

The core differentiator is:

**precise identification, useful specifications, clearly stated condition, and evidence-based verification.**

Do not imply that every item is refurbished or fully function-tested.

Use "refurbished" only where refurbishment has actually been performed and documented.

## Tone

Write in a restrained, technical and confident style.

Prefer:

- precise;
- factual;
- concise;
- technically useful;
- calm;
- specific.

Avoid:

- sales hype;
- exaggerated claims;
- generic marketing language;
- defensive disclaimers;
- speculative warnings;
- "surplus yard" language;
- consumer-style promotional language;
- language that unnecessarily plants doubt.

The page should read as though prepared by a technically competent specialist who knows exactly what the item is.

### BenchSpec voice — apply to every product page

Sound like a knowledgeable seller speaking directly to a buyer, **not** a lab report, technical manual, corporate brochure or legal disclaimer. Buyers generally know the part they need.

- **Use everyday, direct language.** Write `Used, in good physical condition`, not `externally intact` or `visual condition assessed`.
- **Keep sentences short.** Say what the item is, what BenchSpec checked and what the buyer receives. Stop there.
- **Be confident but precise.** `Powered on at 24 V DC with status LEDs checked` is clearer than procedural descriptions of the inspection. Do not imply fully tested operation from a power-on check.
- **Avoid internal language.** Do not carry phrases like `audited batch`, `recorded sale configuration`, `verification boundary`, `disposition` or `hardware-revision grouping` into customer copy.
- **Avoid unnecessarily formal technical phrases.** Use normal product terms for identification and specifications, but don't write `safety-function validation`, `externally intact` or similar when a simpler accurate phrase exists.
- **Don't explain what buyers already know.** Use one brief opening description rather than a general article about the technology.
- **Don't be defensive.** Describe completed checks plainly and stop. A precise positive description of a power-on check is sufficient; do not append a sentence about functionality or safety functions not being tested just because they were not tested. Still disclose actual faults, material exclusions, and specific safety issues where required for an accurate and lawful sale.
- **Don't repeat yourself.** Condition, quantity, inclusions and tests should each have one clear home on the page.

**Voice test:** Would this sound normal if BenchSpec told a technically competent buyer about the part in two sentences? If not, simplify it. Factual accuracy and material disclosures still take precedence over brevity.

## Identification first

Every product page should make the exact identity obvious.

Where available, prominently include:

- manufacturer;
- exact model;
- MPN / ordering code;
- relevant series / sale-defining variant;
- electrical ratings;
- interfaces / connection type;
- key included components;
- BenchSpec reference.

The exact manufacturer/model/MPN should appear naturally in:

- page title;
- H1;
- visible product details;
- meta description;
- structured data.

Do not simplify or shorten an exact identifier where doing so could reduce search relevance or technical clarity.

For several units sold as the same model and series, describe the sale at that level: for example, `Series A. Nine available, sold individually.` Do not discuss internal revision, date-code or country-of-origin groups unless those details materially affect compatibility or the actual offer. Never suggest a buyer can select a variant on eBay unless the live listing supports it.

Keep technical specifications useful and selective, not a copy of the manufacturer's entire data sheet. Preserve relevant ratings, interfaces and safety facts; omit operational inventory detail.

## Condition and verification

Lead with what BenchSpec knows.

Good short-form pattern:

> Used item in very clean physical condition. Tested and operating normally as described below.

Do not use "untested" as the primary item identity when meaningful inspection or partial testing has been completed.

Distinguish clearly between:

1. physical condition;
2. identity/specification verification;
3. functional testing;
4. calibration, where relevant.

State the tests actually performed and their results in plain language.

## Negative statements

Buyer-facing copy should describe **known facts**, not imagined failure modes.

Negative wording is appropriate when it records something specific and real, such as:

- observed damage or wear;
- a missing component;
- an actual failed or negative test result;
- a known material exclusion;
- expired calibration where calibration status matters;
- a specific safety limitation.

Do not add speculative negatives about what might be wrong, might fail, might be incompatible, or might not meet performance that was never measured.

Do not automatically publish every `NOT PERFORMED` audit line. **A positive, scoped test statement stands on its own.** For example: `Used, in good physical condition. Powered on at 24 V DC with status LEDs checked.` Do **not** follow it with `Safety functions have not been tested.` The second sentence adds a negative that is already clear from the first. The same rule applies to other unperformed tests.

Only state an unperformed-test limitation where omitting it would materially mislead a reasonable technical buyer about what BenchSpec verified.

## Verification boundaries

Keep any necessary limitations specific and useful, not defensive.

For example, where a measurement instrument has an expired calibration certificate, that is a known material fact and should be stated.

For safety-critical electrical equipment, state specific voltage or connection limitations when relevant.

Avoid generic wording such as:

> No claim of operation is made.

or broad compatibility disclaimers that are not tied to a known issue.

## Product photography

Use actual-item photography only.

Prefer:

- overall item view;
- manufacturer/model/nameplate;
- important connectors or interfaces;
- included accessories;
- condition details where relevant.

Do not use stock photography or generated substitute images for sale items.

Identification plates should remain readable.

## Homepage / catalogue language

Preferred site positioning:

**BenchSpec**  
**Specialist technical equipment — new surplus, decommissioned and fully specified**

Homepage H1:

**Specialist technical equipment**

Preferred intro:

> Available from stock in Melbourne, with international shipping available. Exact identification, specifications and recorded condition for every item.

Preferred catalogue heading:

**Available equipment**

Avoid "Current equipment" or "Listed equipment" unless there is a specific reason.

## International buyers

Do not make the site feel Australia-only.

Where supported by the actual selling channel, state clearly:

> International shipping available.

If shipping coverage is not confirmed, use:

> International shipping enquiries welcome.

Do not promise worldwide delivery unless it is actually supported.

## Price and availability

Where available, show:

- current asking price;
- currency;
- stock status;
- quantity.

Example:

> A$1,495  
> Available · 1 unit

Keep commercial facts consistent with the live selling channel.

Do not publish internal target prices, floors, acquisition cost, or negotiation notes.

## Warranty and returns

Warranty and return terms are item-specific.

Preferred site-level wording:

> Condition and warranty are stated per item. See each product page for specifications, verification details and current eBay purchase, delivery, returns and warranty terms.

Do not create a blanket warranty promise unless BenchSpec has deliberately adopted one.

Where eBay is the commerce channel, eBay remains the operative source for current transaction, delivery, returns and warranty terms.

## Sold items

Do not delete useful product pages when sold.

Retain:

- permanent URL;
- technical description;
- specifications;
- actual photographs;
- condition record.

Update:

- visible status to Sold;
- structured availability to SoldOut;
- purchase action removed;
- last asking price clearly labelled if retained.

Sold items should not remain in the main available-stock listing.

## Visual style

BenchSpec should feel like a specialist technical catalogue.

Use:

- dark navy / charcoal;
- white or light neutral backgrounds;
- restrained typography;
- subtle borders and rules;
- generous whitespace;
- technical tables;
- strong actual-item photography.

Avoid:

- gradients;
- animation;
- stock imagery;
- bright sales colours;
- excessive badges;
- large marketing hero sections;
- decorative UI;
- ecommerce-style clutter.

The equipment and technical information should dominate.

Use the approved `site/assets/benchspec-logo.svg` in the header, rendered white on the existing navy band. Its logo tagline is **SPECIFIED • VERIFIED**. Keep the longer category descriptor — **Specialist technical equipment — new surplus, decommissioned and fully specified** — as live supporting text. The favicon uses only the icon from that same logo; do not introduce additional decorative brand assets.

## Do / don't examples

### Condition

Do:

> Very clean used condition. Tested at 24 V DC with the supplied controller and operating normally.

Don't:

> Untested. No claim of verified operation is made.

### Negative findings

Do:

> Connector housing has a small crack at the latch; connection remains secure.

> Unit powered on but did not complete startup.

Don't:

> Pressure performance was not tested and may not meet specification.

> Compatibility with other controllers has not been verified.

unless there is a specific known compatibility issue that matters to the offered item.

### Brand tone

Do:

> Exact identification, specifications and recorded condition.

Don't:

> Premium quality industrial solutions at unbeatable prices.

## Product-page review checklist

Before publishing or updating a page, confirm:

- manufacturer/model/MPN is exact;
- item identity matches the audit/source record;
- actual-item photographs are used;
- visible price is current;
- stock status is current;
- international shipping wording is accurate;
- wording passes the plain-English BenchSpec voice test: no audit/report jargon, needless technical formality or repetition;
- internal revision/date/origin tracking is omitted unless relevant to the sale;
- condition wording leads with known facts;
- negative statements describe specific known facts rather than speculative possibilities;
- no routine `not tested` sentence follows a clear positive test statement (including for safety modules); exceptions must address a specific material disclosure, not the mere absence of a test;
- safety warnings are specific and necessary;
- warranty/returns wording is accurate;
- internal commercial information is absent;
- SEO metadata matches visible content;
- structured data matches visible content;
- mobile layout has been reviewed;
- eBay destination is correct;
- sold-state rules are followed where applicable.

## Operating principle

When in doubt:

**state what BenchSpec has confirmed, not everything it has not.**
